# 01. Business Context & Analysis

## 1. Project Context

Vietnam's aquaculture sector is undergoing significant changes driven by technological development, digital transformation, and increasing demands for sustainable production. At the same time, climate change, disease outbreaks, and environmental risks are making aquaculture management increasingly complex.

These changes create a growing need for graduates who not only understand aquaculture theory but can also apply knowledge to practical and data-driven decision-making situations.

Vietnam National University of Agriculture (VNUA), as an institution with strengths in agriculture and aquaculture education, has been investing in digital transformation and Agriculture 4.0 initiatives. However, the analysis conducted in this project identified opportunities to further integrate advanced digital technologies into teaching, practical training, and data utilization.

The project was therefore initiated to explore a digital solution that could support practical learning while creating a foundation for long-term data collection and analysis in the aquaculture domain.

## 2. Industry Context

The aquaculture sector is facing increasing complexity due to environmental changes, disease outbreaks, and the growing need for more effective monitoring and management.

These challenges require future aquaculture professionals to develop not only theoretical knowledge but also practical decision-making capabilities in different farming scenarios.

At the same time, the adoption of digital technologies is creating new opportunities for education and training. Simulation-based learning and technology-enabled training can provide learners with opportunities to experience practical scenarios in a controlled environment.

For aquaculture education, this creates an opportunity to explore new approaches that combine domain knowledge, simulation, and data. Such an approach can support students in practicing decision-making while generating data that can be further analyzed for educational and research purposes.

## 3. Organization Analysis

### 3.1 SWOT Analysis

The SWOT analysis was conducted to assess VNUA's internal capabilities and external environment before proposing a digital solution.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Strong foundation in agriculture and aquaculture education. | Limited specialized resources for applying advanced technologies such as AI, IoT, and Big Data in aquaculture. |
| Existing experience and initiatives related to digital transformation and Agriculture 4.0. | Digital technologies have not yet been fully integrated into practical training and domain-specific data utilization. |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|
| Growing interest in digital transformation and smart agriculture creates opportunities for technology-enabled education. | Increasing competition from other universities investing in technologies such as RAS, AI, and Biofloc. |
| Emerging technologies provide new possibilities for simulation, data collection, and research. | Rapid technological change and rising expectations for practical, industry-oriented education. |

#### -> Key Takeaway

The analysis suggests that VNUA has a strong domain foundation and an existing orientation toward digital transformation. However, there is an opportunity to further integrate digital technologies into practical training and domain-specific data utilization. This creates the strategic context for exploring a technology-enabled solution that combines aquaculture knowledge, simulation, and data.

### 3.2 Porter's Five Forces Analysis

Porter's Five Forces framework was used to assess the external environment and competitive pressures surrounding VNUA and the broader higher education landscape.

| Force | Assessment | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| **Competitive Rivalry** | High | Universities such as Nha Trang University and Nong Lam University are investing in and applying technologies such as RAS, AI, and Biofloc in education, creating increasing competitive pressure. |
| **Threat of New Entrants** | Low to Medium | Establishing a new university requires significant legal and institutional barriers. However, technology companies are increasingly collaborating with universities, creating indirect competitive pressure. |
| **Bargaining Power of Suppliers** | Low | Teaching resources and academic materials are relatively available, and switching costs are low. However, advanced technology equipment may still depend on international suppliers. |
| **Bargaining Power of Customers** | High | Students increasingly expect practical and application-oriented learning environments. This creates pressure for universities to adopt smarter and more technology-enabled approaches to education. |
| **Threat of Substitutes** | Medium | Online learning platforms, virtual reality training, short courses, and corporate training programs provide alternative ways for learners to acquire practical and professional knowledge. |

#### -> Key Takeaway

The analysis shows that VNUA operates in an increasingly competitive educational environment where students have higher expectations for practical and technology-enabled learning experiences.

While the barriers to establishing new universities remain relatively high, competition is also coming from alternative education models and technology-enabled training solutions. This creates a strategic need for VNUA to further differentiate its educational offerings by integrating technology into practical learning and strengthening its ability to provide application-oriented training.

### 3.3 Digital Maturity Assessment

To assess VNUA's current level of digital transformation, the project used a Digital Maturity Map (DMM).

Based on the analysis, VNUA is currently positioned at the **Digitization** stage of digital maturity.

The university has already established a foundation for digital transformation through:

- Investment in information technology systems supporting education management and administrative operations.
- Existing programs and initiatives related to digital transformation and Agriculture 4.0.
- A dedicated IT workforce responsible for operating and maintaining digital systems.
- Basic digital skills among lecturers, staff, and students.
- Basic cybersecurity measures for protecting systems and student data.
- Data collection from existing management systems for administrative management and statistical reporting.

However, the current use of digital technology and data remains primarily focused on operational and administrative purposes. There is an opportunity to further develop data-driven applications that support teaching, practical learning, research, and decision-making.

#### -> Key Takeaway

The assessment indicates that VNUA has already established the fundamental capabilities required for digital transformation. The next opportunity is to move beyond basic digitization by applying technology and data more directly to core educational and research activities.

This creates a suitable context for exploring a solution that combines **domain knowledge, digital simulation, and data generation** to support more practical and data-driven learning experiences.

## 4. Key Business Challenges

Based on the internal and external analyses, the project identified several key challenges and opportunities for VNUA.

### Challenge 1: Increasing demand for practical and technology-enabled learning

Students increasingly expect learning experiences that provide practical application rather than theoretical knowledge alone. At the same time, alternative learning models and technology-enabled training solutions are becoming more widely available.

This creates pressure for VNUA to strengthen the practical and application-oriented aspects of its educational offerings.

### Challenge 2: Increasing competitive pressure in technology-enabled education

Other universities are investing in and applying technologies such as RAS, AI, and Biofloc in education and research.

To remain competitive, VNUA has an opportunity to further differentiate its educational offerings through the application of digital technologies in domain-specific learning and training.

### Challenge 3: Limited integration of advanced technologies into practical training

Although VNUA has an existing foundation in digital transformation and Agriculture 4.0, the analysis identified limitations in the integration of advanced technologies such as AI, IoT, and Big Data into aquaculture-related activities.

There is therefore an opportunity to explore how digital technologies can be applied more directly to practical training and domain-specific learning.

### Challenge 4: Underutilization of data for core educational and research activities

The Digital Maturity Assessment showed that VNUA has established systems for collecting and managing data. However, data utilization remains largely focused on administrative management and statistical reporting.

The project identified an opportunity to generate and utilize data more strategically to support learning performance analysis, research, and future data-driven applications.

#### -> Overall Business Need

The analyses indicate that VNUA has an existing foundation for digital transformation but also faces increasing pressure to provide more practical, technology-enabled, and data-driven educational experiences.

Therefore, the key business need identified by the project was to explore a digital solution that could simultaneously support practical learning, encourage decision-making in realistic scenarios, and create opportunities for long-term data generation and utilization.

## 5. Project Objectives

Based on the identified business challenges, the project aimed to explore a digital transformation solution that could strengthen practical learning in aquaculture while creating opportunities for long-term data utilization.

The project objectives were defined as follows:

### 1. Enhance practical learning experiences

Provide students with a simulated environment where they can apply aquaculture knowledge and practice decision-making across different shrimp farming scenarios without the risks and costs associated with real-world experimentation.

### 2. Support performance-based learning and assessment

Create a mechanism for recording user decisions and evaluating simulation outcomes based on relevant performance indicators, such as productivity, survival rate, costs, and profitability.

This allows the learning process to be assessed based not only on theoretical knowledge but also on decision-making and simulated outcomes.

### 3. Create a foundation for data collection and analysis

Capture data generated throughout simulation sessions, including user interactions, decisions, and outcomes.

The objective is to create a structured data foundation that can support performance analysis, research, and future data-driven applications.

### 4. Explore the potential for a long-term aquaculture data ecosystem

Beyond its role as an educational simulation platform, the project explores the potential to combine simulation-generated data with other relevant aquaculture data sources.

This creates a foundation for the project's long-term Data Moat strategy and future applications in analytics, research, and AI.

## 6. Key Findings

The business and organizational analyses highlighted several key findings that shaped the direction of the project.

First, the aquaculture sector is becoming increasingly complex due to environmental risks and disease outbreaks, creating a greater need for practical and decision-oriented learning experiences.

Second, VNUA operates in an increasingly competitive educational environment where students expect more practical and technology-enabled learning opportunities.

Third, VNUA has established a foundation for digital transformation through existing IT infrastructure, digital initiatives, and Agriculture 4.0 development. However, the analysis identified an opportunity to further integrate digital technologies and data into core educational and research activities.

Finally, the project identified the potential to use simulation not only as a learning tool but also as a mechanism for generating structured data from user decisions and simulation outcomes.

### Business Implication

Based on these findings, the project identified an opportunity to explore a digital solution that could address multiple needs simultaneously:

- Provide a practical and low-risk learning environment for aquaculture students.
- Enable learners to practice decision-making in different farming scenarios.
- Support performance evaluation based on user decisions and simulation outcomes.
- Generate structured data that could support future analytics, research, and data-driven applications.

These findings provided the foundation for the proposed solution: **ShrimpSim – a shrimp farming simulation platform designed to combine simulation-based learning with long-term data generation and utilization.**
